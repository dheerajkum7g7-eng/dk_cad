What’s new in 4.1

  • A real compass needle. The direction is now a sharp red needle — its point is the way to walk — and it turns smoothly as you turn on the spot, instead of the vague triangle it was before.
  • The distance moves with every step. DK CAD now asks Android for the GPS itself, twice a second, with no distance filter at all. So the number counts down as you walk — 30, 29, 28 — instead of sitting still and then jumping twenty metres at once.
  • Measured on the drawing’s own grid. The metres are worked out on the survey grid your drawing is drawn on, in full precision, rather than on an average sphere. An exact 100.000 m now reads 100.000 m; it used to read 100.5. A coarse GPS fix is also thrown out rather than allowed to drag the target sideways.
  • The cross closes the card. It was being swallowed by the panel behind it, so the only way out was the back button. Tapping it now stops the GPS and returns you to the drawing.

Also new in 4.0

  • Walk to any object — inside DK CAD. Tap 🎯 Locate in the bottom bar, then tap a point, block, line, text or corner. A card appears with a big arrow and the distance in metres. Hold the phone up, walk the way the arrow points, and the number counts down as you go — 30 m, 20 m, 15 m — until it says you are here.
  • No maps app, no roads, no route. Nothing opens outside DK CAD. It does not tell you which street to take or whether to walk or drive — just the direction and how far, which is what you want on a site.
  • The arrow turns with the phone. Turn on the spot and it swings to keep pointing at the target, so the arrow is always “that way from where you are standing right now”.
  • It tells you when the GPS cannot keep up. If your fix is coarser than the distance you have left, it says so instead of pretending the last few metres are exact. Close the card with the cross and the GPS is released.
  • It refuses rather than guesses. A drawing on a local 0,0 site grid, or a title block in millimetres, is not a place on Earth — DK CAD says so, with the reason, instead of pointing you somewhere wrong.

Also new in 3.8

  • Labels belong to the drawing now. The X/Y and distance labels you plot grow and shrink with the drawing as you zoom, exactly the way text inside a CAD drawing does — so a label always covers the same part of the plan instead of swelling over it when you zoom out.
  • You set how big. Type a Label scale in the bottom bar, anywhere from 0.001 to 10. 1 is the standard label; on a survey plan you will usually want well below that. It is a scale, not a height, so the same number means the same thing in a metre survey and a millimetre detail — and it is remembered next time you open the app.

Also new in 3.5

  • Less than half the download. The app was carrying four copies of its engine — including two that only ever run on a PC emulator, never on a phone. Now it ships only what your phone actually uses: about 40 MB instead of 92 MB. Nothing on the phone behaves differently.

Also new in 3.4

  • White or black drawing sheet. Switch with one button in the bottom bar. On the white sheet, white lines and text are drawn dark automatically, so nothing vanishes into the page — the rest of the drawing keeps its own colours.
  • Find text. Type any word or part of one and DK CAD jumps straight to it, zoomed in with the match boxed in orange. Steps through every hit with the arrows.
  • Find reads what you see. MText that is stored with formatting codes still matches on the words on screen, and dimension text and block labels are searched too.

Installing

  1. Tap Download for Android above.
  2. Chrome will warn that this file type can harm your device — that warning appears for every APK that did not come from the Play Store. Choose Download anyway.
  3. Open the downloaded file. Android will ask permission to install from this source — allow it once.
  4. Tap Install. Installing over an older DK CAD keeps your recent files.

Already have DK CAD installed? Just install this on top — nothing needs to be uninstalled first.

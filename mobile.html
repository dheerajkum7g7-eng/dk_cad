What’s new in 6.9 — heavy drawings open again

  • A drawing built from a very large block (a bound site plan is one block of ten million line segments) stopped opening in 6.7 and closed the app. Measured cause: 6.7 gave every block segment its own colour, held three times as a list eight bytes per segment — some 260 MB on top of the 350 MB the segments take, more than the phone allows an app.
  • Colours are now kept as runs (ten million segments are a few hundred runs); a block above two million segments never holds a per-segment list and is drawn run by run; the model box list is no longer duplicated for the sheet view. The picture is the same.
  • One honest note on the first open: 6.6 and 6.7 changed what the saved copy carries, so a heavy drawing’s first open after those updates re-reads the file in full — tens of seconds on a very large one — and writes a new copy; every open after that is seconds again. 6.8 keeps the drawing file beside its copy so that re-read happens by itself.

Also new in 6.8 — an update shows its new picture at once

  • Why an update looked as if it had not installed. After updating, the app reopened your last drawing from the saved copy the previous build had made — and that copy carried none of what the new build adds (layout tabs, block colours). Uninstalling happened to clear the copy, which is why that “fixed” it.
  • The drawing file is now kept beside its saved copy. When a later build changes the copy format, the drawing is re-read from that file at native speed — seconds — and the new picture appears. Nothing is deleted; the Recent list is untouched; the storage budget counts the kept files.
  • A copy from an older build with no file beside it (every drawing opened before this build) is no longer reopened silently at launch — the home screen tells you to open that drawing once with Open, after which it is kept with its file.

Also new in 6.7 — layouts measured against AutoCAD

  • If the layout tabs did not appear for you: the drawing was reopened from a saved copy made by an older build, and that copy carries no sheets. Open the drawing once more with the Open button — a fresh copy is made in seconds and the Model / Layout tabs appear; Recent works from then on. The status bar now says so.
  • Thirty sheets plotted in AutoCAD and compared ink for ink. That found five more things: a viewport keeps its own layer (a drawing with layer 0 off no longer loses every window); a viewport whose layer is off loses only its border, only a frozen layer hides it; objects inside a block are drawn in their own colours; multi-line text is placed by its attachment point, wrapped and centred as the drawing says; and the white page is placed from the plot window when the drawing’s limits are stale.
  • Measured: the AutoCAD ink we also paint went from 69% to 92% on one sheet, 80% to 96% on another, 72% to 94% on three more.
  • Opening speed untouched — reader and app rebuilt together.

Also new in 6.6 — layout tabs, done AutoCAD’s way

  • The Model / Layout tabs are back, rebuilt from the ground up after 6.0 showed the wrong content. Four real causes were found — measured on your own drawings and against AutoCAD itself plotting the same sheets: every layout carries a hidden viewport that is the paper-space view, and it was being taken for a window into the model, so the whole drawing was drawn over the sheet; a rotated (twisted) viewport was read in the wrong unit, the wrong sign and the wrong frame; a window that showed little was silently replaced by a fit of the whole model; and hatches, dimensions, points and block fills were never drawn through a window at all.
  • A viewport is now a live window. The app clips to its boundary — rectangular or shaped — and draws the model through it at the sheet’s scale and rotation, so hatches, dimensions, text, blocks and points appear exactly as they do in model space. Layers frozen in that viewport only are honoured. The white page and its printable border sit under everything.
  • Checked against AutoCAD: a title block with a hatched excavation through its window, a survey sheet with rotated windows, a sheet with sixteen windows and one with five shaped windows — all match.
  • Road bands: a polyline drawn with a width is now a band on the phone, not a hairline.
  • Opening speed untouched. The fast native reader and the app were rebuilt together; your saved drawings are re-read once, at native speed, to pick the sheets up.

Also new in 6.5 — hatches on the phone, two more fixes

  • The invisible grey hatch. A pattern with several parallel line families at different base points (GOST_GROUND, AR-PARQ1 and the like) collapsed into one family — a third of its lines — so a dark-coloured one could not be seen. The families are now drawn where the pattern puts them, and a dark colour on the dark sheet is lifted just enough to read.
  • Scale when zoomed out. A pattern used to look coarser than the drawing around it, because the rows that could not be drawn were simply dropped. Their ink is now put back as a tint, so a hatch darkens as it shrinks — the way it does in AutoCAD.

Also new in 6.4 — one tap

  • The app icon is your drawing. DK CAD now reopens whatever you had open last, straight from its saved copy — seconds — instead of landing on the file list and making you pick it again every time. A switch on the home page turns it off if you prefer starting on the list, and anything you tap outside the app always wins over it.
  • Tapping a drawing in a file manager works. File managers hand a drawing over with whatever type their own table happens to hold, and for a DWG that is often nothing useful — so DK CAD did not appear in the list at all. It now accepts the handover whatever type is claimed and checks the first bytes itself, refusing politely if it is not a drawing.
  • Sharing into DK CAD works too. A share from WhatsApp, Gmail or Drive is a different kind of request from a tap, and the app was never listening for it — so it was missing from the share sheet. It is there now.

Also new in 6.3

  • Hatch scale — the real cause, and it was not the renderer. On a phone a DWG is always read through a saved copy: the fast native reader parses the drawing, writes that copy, and the app loads it. The hatch’s own pattern definition — the line families the drawing carries, which state the spacing, the angle and the dash lengths — was never written into that copy. Every hatch therefore arrived on the device with no definition and fell back to a small built-in table looked up by pattern name, whose base unit is imperial: wrong spacing, wrong angle, and nothing drawn at all for a pattern the table has never heard of. On a computer the same drawing measured perfectly, because there the reader’s own result is used directly — which is exactly why this took so long to find.
  • It is carried now, blocks included — and a check fails the build if it is ever dropped again. Measured end to end on a real drawing: 33 of 33 pattern hatches arrive with the drawing’s own families, at the row spacing the file states.
  • Your Recent list is safe again. The previous build cleared saved copies once, and because a Recent entry points at its saved copy, the whole list emptied itself after the update. Fixed twice over: copies written by older builds are now read rather than refused, and a Recent entry is never deleted just because its copy is gone — it tells you to open the drawing once and it is quick again. Nothing is deleted automatically in this build.
  • Opening speed untouched. The format the fast reader writes and the format the app reads were changed together in this build — the one rule that matters here, and the one 6.0 broke.

Also new in 6.2

  • Hatch stops at its boundary — curves included. The remaining leak was not the curves at all: it was the order the boundary edges are stored in. A drawing may store an outline’s edges in any order and in either direction, and the app was stringing them together as they came — which turns a simple outline into a figure that crosses itself, and a self-crossing outline fills as a wedge right across the drawing. The edges are now walked by their endpoints: each step takes the edge that really continues the outline, turned round when it is joined by its far end. Measured on a square whose edges are stored out of order: read as stored it encloses half the area it should; walked, it is exact.
  • Curves keep 6.1’s accuracy. A bulged polyline edge, an elliptical edge and a spline edge are each followed as a curve, to three parts in a thousand of the radius — and every hatch uses the pattern definition the drawing itself carries, so spacing, angle and dash lengths are the file’s own numbers.
  • Opening speed is untouched. This is boundary work, not reading work: on a 200-hatch drawing the entire hatch boundary set is under 7,000 points and the conversion step is 0.8 s of a 5.2 s open — the rest is the DWG reader, which is unchanged, and the cache format still matches what the fast native reader writes.
  • Still open: hatches vanishing as you zoom in is not reproduced here — a large hatch on survey coordinates stays drawn a million times in, and fourteen deep-zoom redraws cost 53 ms. That one needs the drawing it happens on.

Also new in 6.1 — the speed repair

  • Speed is back. 6.0 opened drawings through the slow reader by mistake — a simple drawing took twenty seconds, a heavy one sat for minutes. The fast native DWG reader writes its result in a versioned format, and 6.0 raised that version without rebuilding the reader, so every conversion was discarded as unreadable and the app fell back to the slow path it only keeps for phones that cannot run the fast one. The format matches again, the reader has been rebuilt from current code, and a check now fails the build if the two ever disagree.
  • Hatch really does stop at curved boundaries now. The bleeding was inside that same native reader: it took a curved boundary edge as the straight line across it, and ignored elliptical and spline edges completely. It now follows the curve to three parts in a thousand of the radius — and carries each hatch’s own pattern definition, so spacing, angle and dash lengths are the drawing’s own numbers.
  • Your saved drawings are re-read once. Anything already opened on the phone kept whatever the old reader made of it, so this release clears those saved results one time. The next open of each drawing re-reads it at native speed — seconds — and is instant from then on.
  • Layout tabs are switched off. The Model/Layout sheet tabs from 6.0 were showing the wrong content, so they are disabled until that is worked through properly. Layers and everything from 5.9 and earlier are unchanged.
  • Still open, said plainly: hatches disappearing as you zoom in has not been reproduced here yet — not by the pattern anchor, not by the culling, and not by cost (fourteen deep-zoom redraws of a large hatch take 53 ms). If it still happens on this build, the drawing itself is what is needed to find it.

Also new in 6.0

  • ☰ Layers. A new Layers button slides a panel up from the bottom listing every layer in the drawing — its own colour, how many objects sit on it, and an eye that switches it off. Tap a row to hide a layer, Iso to show only that one, or All on / All off; a filter box handles the survey drawings that carry a hundred and fifty layers. What you have hidden is shown on the button itself, so half a missing drawing can never be a mystery — and a tap can no longer pick or erase something on a switched-off layer, because you cannot select what the drawing is not showing.
  • Model and Layout tabs (paper space). A drawing’s sheets now appear as tabs above the bottom bar — Model first, then every layout the file carries. Tapping one opens that sheet: its title block, its notes, and the drawing seen through each of its viewports, at the sheet’s own scale and clipped to the window. A sheet with nothing on it says so before you open it, and Model space is untouched by the trip — switching back gives you the same drawing you left, with your work on it.
  • Hatches: three real bugs, fixed. Shading used to bleed past curved boundaries, because a curved edge was being taken as the straight line across it — the bulged edges of a polyline, and ellipse and spline edges, were not being read at all. (In one of your own drawings, 185 of its 200 hatches have a curved edge.) They are now followed as curves.
  • …and the pattern is the file’s own. The size was wrong for the same kind of reason: each pattern was looked up by name in a small built-in table instead of reading the definition the drawing itself carries, so the spacing, the angle and the dash and gap lengths were an approximation of a different pattern. The drawing’s own definition is now used, at the CAD spacing it states — and a hatch too small to show its pattern no longer draws every row outside itself and appears as an empty outline.

Also new in 5.9

  • “GPS ±100 m” was never GPS. A fix that never got better than about a hundred metres was a cell-tower and Wi-Fi position — which the app receives while the satellite chip is still searching, and then displayed with the word GPS in front of it. The number was real; the word was wrong, and nothing on screen let you tell. Every position is now labelled by what actually produced it: GPS ±4 m, NETWORK ±100 m — not a GPS fix, or an external receiver — and the app tells you the one thing that fixes it: step into the open.
  • A tower position can no longer become a coordinate. It is refused for measuring with, whatever accuracy it claims for itself, because a hundred beautifully consistent tower guesses are still a hundred metres out.
  • Precise vs Approximate location. Android lets a permission be granted as Approximate, and in that state the phone deliberately rounds your position to roughly a hundred metres — no app can override it. DK CAD now checks which of the two it actually holds, says so plainly, and opens the exact settings page that holds the switch.
  • 📍 Here now averages. Instead of one instant reading, hold the phone still and it takes every fix for thirty seconds, throws out any wild one, weights the good ones by their own accuracy, and records the average with the honest ± it achieved stored next to the point. Tap ⏹ Stop to take it sooner. In the open this typically turns a five-metre reading into one or two.
  • Said honestly: averaging removes the random half of GPS error, not the rest, so DK CAD will never print a precision the phone cannot support. Genuine sub-metre needs an external RTK receiver — and if one is feeding your phone, DK CAD now recognises it and reports its centimetres instead of ignoring it.

Also new in 5.8

  • The update notice is instant. It used to start only after the whole app had finished loading, and then asked each server one after the other — so the notice could arrive ten or fifteen seconds in, by which time you had already opened a drawing in the old build and stopped looking. It now starts on the first instruction of a launch, while the screen is still being built, and asks every server at the same time. It still appears only on a fresh start, never when you come back to the app from something else.
  • GPS that is switched off now says so. Giving DK CAD permission and having the phone’s location actually turned on are two different switches, and only the first was ever checked. With permission granted but the phone’s GPS off, every position path failed quietly and Locate simply sat on “waiting for a GPS fix” forever, with nothing on screen to say why. It now notices, tells you exactly what is wrong, and takes you straight to the setting to turn it on.
  • Locate no longer starts from an old position. The first position shown was whatever the phone had last recorded, with no check of how old it was — this morning, or another town. So Locate pointed somewhere wrong for ten to fifteen seconds and then jumped. A remembered position is now used only while it is still seconds old and accurate; otherwise DK CAD goes and gets a real fix at full accuracy. When there is genuinely nothing recent it says waiting for a GPS fix, which is true, instead of showing a confident wrong number.

Also new in 5.4

  • Local to UTM. A drawing on a local site grid (starting at 0,0 or 1000,1000) states nothing about where it is on Earth. Now you can tell DK CAD where it is — and it can often work it out by itself, by reading the drawing’s own written coordinate labels. Each coordinate is taken from the survey marker or the leader arrowhead the label points at, never from the text box, which on a survey sheet routinely sits tens of metres away from the point it describes. Every label is cross-checked against the others and any that disagree are thrown out, so one typo cannot tilt the answer.
  • Or set it by hand. If the drawing carries no written coordinates, pick two points and type what they really are. A third is offered, because it is the first point that can check the other two. Nothing is applied until you have seen the rotation, the scale and the error it fits with, and the alignment is remembered for that drawing.
  • Background map. A new Map button puts satellite imagery, a road map or a topographic map behind your drawing, lined up with the real world — so your lines sit on the ground they were surveyed from. One tap cycles Satellite, Road, Topo and off. It stays off until you switch it on, and it never switches itself on again, not even for the next drawing.
  • It asks which zone, and shows you. The map asks which UTM zone the drawing is in instead of assuming the zone you happen to be standing in, and it shows you where that zone puts the drawing, so a wrong one is obvious before you accept it. Zooming in deeply no longer leaves blank tiles: where the imagery runs out, the closest level that does exist is stretched instead, so the map is never white. Tiles are cached on the phone, so panning back over ground you have already seen costs no data.
  • Imagery credit: Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics. Road map by OpenStreetMap contributors.

Also new in 5.1

  • Label height is an absolute value again. The box in the bottom bar opens at exactly 1.0 in every drawing and accepts anything from 0.0001 to 100. It is no longer calculated from the drawing’s extents, so it will not open at 730 in one file and 0.0075 in another, and there is no per-drawing minimum to get stuck against.
  • The coordinate leader stays put. Its direction is fixed at the moment the point is picked, so the arrow and its text no longer flip from one side to the other as you zoom.
  • The UTM picker now snaps. Endpoints, midpoints, intersections, nodes — and geometry inside blocks — exactly like the drawing tools.
  • A complete readout for every point. The panel shows the drawing’s own X,Y, the UTM easting/northing with the zone and MGRS band (for example 43Q) worked out from the point itself, and the WGS84 latitude and longitude.
  • Georeferenced drawings are recognised properly. Whether a drawing is on a survey grid is now decided by a vote of its geometry rather than by the centre of its bounding box, so a single stray object at the origin no longer causes a valid survey to be reported as local. A genuinely local drawing is labelled Local Coordinate and is given no UTM zone at all.

Also new in 5.0

  • You, on the drawing. Press 📡 GPS and a blue dot marks your own position on the plan, with a circle showing how accurate the satellite fix is. Walk, and the dot moves — exactly like Google Maps, but on your CAD drawing.
  • Anywhere in the world. The UTM zone and the hemisphere are worked out from your own position, so it is correct in India, Australia or Peru without changing a setting.
  • It refuses rather than misleads. On a local site grid (0,0-based coordinates, a millimetre detail, a title block) there is no honest place for you on the sheet, and it says so instead of putting the dot somewhere wrong.
  • 📍 Here. A new button in the UTM panel drops your current position into the coordinate list and copies its Easting/Northing and latitude/longitude straight to the clipboard.
  • Labels are CAD text again. The box in the bottom bar is now a height in drawing units: type 1 on a metre survey and the label is one metre tall, growing and shrinking with the drawing as you zoom, the way an AutoCAD text height does — instead of staying one size on screen and covering the plan when you zoom out.

Also new in 4.9

  • UTM & Lat/Long picker. Press 🌐 UTM and tap the drawing as many times as you like. Every tap drops a numbered pin exactly where you touched and adds a line underneath with its Easting/Northing and its latitude and longitude.
  • Copy All. One button puts the whole list on the clipboard: it pastes into Excel as columns, reads perfectly well in WhatsApp, and the lat/long lines paste straight into Google Earth or Maps. There is an undo for the last pin and a Clear for the lot — and it never touches your plotted markers.
  • Where the numbers are honest. A drawing on a local 0,0 site grid has no true latitude, so the point keeps its Easting/Northing and says why there is no lat/long, instead of inventing a position somewhere wrong.
  • Snapping reaches inside blocks at last. Endpoints, midpoints, intersections and nodes of the lines within a block symbol can now be picked — before, a block was invisible to snapping however close your finger was.
  • New INS snap. Switch on INS in the snap row to snap to the insertion point of TEXT and MTEXT, including labels and attributes inside blocks.
  • The coordinate and distance labels are readable again. They were multiplied by the zoom, so at a working zoom one X= line covered the whole drawing and even a scale of 0.001 could not hold it back. They are now a fixed size on screen at any zoom, and they stay beside the point you tapped rather than being shoved into a corner. The box in the bottom bar is now simply how big that text is.
  • A slimmer, three-row toolbar. Global actions on top, every drawing tool as its own button in a row that slides sideways (no more menu, no more two taps per tool), then the snap toggles. Smaller text and tighter spacing throughout — noticeably more of the screen is your drawing.

Also new in 4.3

  • Locate goes to the spot you touched. Until now a tap was pulled onto whatever was drawn nearby — a text jumped to its insertion point, a line to its nearest corner — so the place you meant and the place the arrow counted down to were not the same, and the gap grew the further out you were zoomed. The target is now the coordinate under your finger, full stop.
  • A crosshair on the exact point. The tapped coordinate is marked with a cyan crosshair, so you can see precisely which point the distance and the arrow are working from. Tap again anywhere to move it; press 🎯 Locate again to stop.
  • Everything else is unchanged. The distance, the arrow and how often the satellite reading updates all work exactly as they did in 4.2.

Also new in 4.2

  • The metres move with every single pace. The count used to show whole metres once you were more than ten away, so a step forward at 28 m still read “28 m” and the screen looked stuck while you were plainly walking. It now carries a decimal all the way out to 100 m — 28.4, 27.8, 27.2 — so every pace shows. Being a real satellite reading, the last digit will fidget by a metre or so while you stand still; that is the GPS being shown honestly rather than hidden.
  • Tap anywhere, not just on something drawn. The place you need to reach is often not an object — the corner of a plot, a spot between two poles. Any bare patch of the drawing is now a valid destination.
  • You can see what you picked. The destination gets a cyan ring and crosshair on the drawing, and if you tapped a text, block or line, that object is outlined too — so it is obvious you got the one you meant and not its neighbour.
  • Keep tapping to retarget. Locate now stays on after a tap, so each new tap moves the target. Panning and pinching are unaffected. Press 🎯 Locate again to stop.

Also new in 4.1

  • A real compass needle. The direction is now a sharp red needle — its point is the way to walk — and it turns smoothly as you turn on the spot, instead of the vague triangle it was before.
  • The distance moves with every step. DK CAD now asks Android for the GPS itself, twice a second, with no distance filter at all. So the number counts down as you walk — 30, 29, 28 — instead of sitting still and then jumping twenty metres at once.
  • Measured on the drawing’s own grid. The metres are worked out on the survey grid your drawing is drawn on, in full precision, rather than on an average sphere. An exact 100.000 m now reads 100.000 m; it used to read 100.5. A coarse GPS fix is also thrown out rather than allowed to drag the target sideways.
  • The cross closes the card. It was being swallowed by the panel behind it, so the only way out was the back button. Tapping it now stops the GPS and returns you to the drawing.

Also new in 4.0

  • Walk to any object — inside DK CAD. Tap 🎯 Locate in the bottom bar, then tap a point, block, line, text or corner. A card appears with a big arrow and the distance in metres. Hold the phone up, walk the way the arrow points, and the number counts down as you go — 30 m, 20 m, 15 m — until it says you are here.
  • No maps app, no roads, no route. Nothing opens outside DK CAD. It does not tell you which street to take or whether to walk or drive — just the direction and how far, which is what you want on a site.
  • The arrow turns with the phone. Turn on the spot and it swings to keep pointing at the target, so the arrow is always “that way from where you are standing right now”.
  • It tells you when the GPS cannot keep up. If your fix is coarser than the distance you have left, it says so instead of pretending the last few metres are exact. Close the card with the cross and the GPS is released.
  • It refuses rather than guesses. A drawing on a local 0,0 site grid, or a title block in millimetres, is not a place on Earth — DK CAD says so, with the reason, instead of pointing you somewhere wrong.

Also new in 3.8

  • Labels belong to the drawing now. The X/Y and distance labels you plot grow and shrink with the drawing as you zoom, exactly the way text inside a CAD drawing does — so a label always covers the same part of the plan instead of swelling over it when you zoom out.
  • You set how big. Type a Label scale in the bottom bar, anywhere from 0.001 to 10. 1 is the standard label; on a survey plan you will usually want well below that. It is a scale, not a height, so the same number means the same thing in a metre survey and a millimetre detail — and it is remembered next time you open the app.

Also new in 3.5

  • Less than half the download. The app was carrying four copies of its engine — including two that only ever run on a PC emulator, never on a phone. Now it ships only what your phone actually uses: about 40 MB instead of 92 MB. Nothing on the phone behaves differently.

Also new in 3.4

  • White or black drawing sheet. Switch with one button in the bottom bar. On the white sheet, white lines and text are drawn dark automatically, so nothing vanishes into the page — the rest of the drawing keeps its own colours.
  • Find text. Type any word or part of one and DK CAD jumps straight to it, zoomed in with the match boxed in orange. Steps through every hit with the arrows.

Installing

  1. Tap Download for Android above.
  2. Chrome will warn that this file type can harm your device — that warning appears for every APK that did not come from the Play Store. Choose Download anyway.
  3. Open the downloaded file. Android will ask permission to install from this source — allow it once.
  4. Tap Install. Installing over an older DK CAD keeps your recent files.

Already have DK CAD installed? Just install this on top — nothing needs to be uninstalled first.
